Literature Triage Agent

AI agents are now sold for screening papers for literature reviews and regulatory filings. They're fast, but they follow rules, and rules miss things. In this lab an agent screens ten abstracts for a review. Your job is to check its work, then try to make it better.

  • Check an agent's decisions against clear inclusion criteria.
  • Spot the kinds of mistakes rule-following agents make: negation, synonyms, study type, retractions.
  • Measure an agent with precision and recall, and see why "just add another rule" has limits.
Everything here is fictional. "Velorastat," "VLR-201," and the kinase "KX1" are made up, and so are all ten abstracts. That lets us build in realistic traps without misinforming anyone about a real drug.

The review question

Does Velorastat inhibit the kinase KX1 in human cells?

  • Include: primary experimental studies that test Velorastat (also called VLR-201) on KX1 in human cells, whatever the result. Negative results count as evidence too.
  • Exclude: review articles, animal-only studies, computer-only (in silico) studies, studies of other compounds, and retracted papers.
Reflect and discuss
  • The agent got some papers right for the wrong reason. Why is that a problem even when the final answer is correct?
  • For a review, which mistake is worse: including a bad paper (low precision) or missing a good one (low recall)? Would your answer change for a safety review?
  • Today's AI agents use language models rather than keyword rules, so they handle synonyms better. Which of the traps here could still catch them?
  • What would a sensible human-checking step look like in your workplace if an agent did the first pass?
