Can This Data Cross the Border?

AI in biology runs on data, and more and more rules decide where that data may go. A Seattle university and a partner university in Shenzhen want to run a joint AI-for-biology course. For each request below, decide: share it, get a review first, or don't share it.

  • Recognize which kinds of data carry cross-border risk: human genetic data, health data, personal data, and data tied to restricted companies.
  • See why "methods, not data" keeps international teaching collaborations simple.
  • Know when to stop and ask your compliance or research office.
This is not legal advice. The rules summarized here are simplified for teaching and change often. Real decisions belong with your institution's compliance, export-control, or research-integrity office.

The rules in play

  • China's human genetic resources rules require government approval before human genetic samples or data collected in China go to foreign parties.
  • US rules on bulk sensitive data restrict giving large amounts of Americans' genomic, health, and other sensitive personal data to people and companies in countries of concern, including China. De-identifying the data doesn't remove the restriction.
  • US trade restrictions and biosecurity legislation name certain Chinese biotech companies. Working with them can bring legal and funding risk.
  • Student and personal data is protected on both sides: FERPA in the US and the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) in China.
  • Public and synthetic data are the safe default for teaching.
Reflect and discuss
  • Which scenario surprised you most? Why?
  • Who in your organization would you ask about a cross-border data question? Do you know their name?
  • How could you design a joint project so that no sensitive data ever needs to move?
